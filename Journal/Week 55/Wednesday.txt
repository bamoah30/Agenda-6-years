ROBOTICS ROADMAP
--------------------------------------------------
Book Read:
- Modern Robotics, Chapter 2

Lessons:
- Holonomic Constraints:
  Constraints that can be expressed as equations involving only the robot's configuration variables (position and orientation). They directly reduce the number of independent motions available to the robot.

- Non-Holonomic Constraints:
  Constraints that depend on the robot's velocity and cannot be written purely in terms of position. These constraints limit how a robot can move but do not directly reduce the robot's configuration space. A common example is a car, which can move forward and backward but cannot move sideways.

- Pfaffian Constraints:
  A mathematical representation of motion constraints written in the form A(q)q̇ = 0, where q represents the robot's configuration and q̇ its velocity. They are commonly used to describe non-holonomic systems such as wheeled mobile robots.

Key Takeaways:
- Holonomic constraints restrict the robot's configuration directly.
- Non-holonomic constraints restrict the robot's allowable velocities.
- Many mobile robots are non-holonomic because their wheels prevent certain directions of motion.
- Pfaffian constraints provide a compact mathematical way to model robot motion restrictions.

Definitions:
- Free-Body Diagram (FBD):
  A drawing that isolates a body and shows all external forces and moments acting on it.

- Equilibrium Equations:
  Mathematical equations stating that the sum of forces and the sum of moments acting on a body must be zero when the body is in equilibrium.
