DC CIRCUIT ANALYSIS

Key Idea:
When analysing DC circuits, voltage can be thought of as the electrical force or energy that drives current through a circuit.

Notes:
- Voltage provides the potential difference that allows current to flow.
- Circuit components (such as resistors, lamps, and motors) cause voltage drops as electrical energy is transferred or used.

------------------------------------------------------------

KIRCHHOFF'S VOLTAGE LAW (KVL)

Definition:
The algebraic sum of all voltage rises and voltage drops around any closed loop (mesh) in a circuit is zero.

Mathematically:
ΣV = 0

Interpretation:
- Total voltage supplied = Total voltage consumed
- Energy is conserved around a closed loop.

Example:
For a loop containing a 12 V source and two resistors with voltage drops of 5 V and 7 V:

+12 - 5 - 7 = 0

Therefore, KVL is satisfied.

VOLTAGE DROP

Definition:
The voltage drop across a component is the difference between the voltage at the point where current enters the component and the voltage at the point where current leaves the component.

Formula:
Voltage Drop = V(start) - V(end)

Where:
- V(start) = Voltage at the current-entry terminal of the component.
- V(end) = Voltage at the current-exit terminal of the component.

Example:
If current enters a resistor at 12 V and leaves at 8 V:

Voltage Drop = 12 - 8 = 4 V

Therefore, the resistor has a voltage drop of 4 V.

QUICK SUMMARY

1. Voltage is the electrical force or energy that drives current through a circuit.

2. Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL):
   ΣV = 0
   The total voltage rises and drops around any closed loop sum to zero.

3. Voltage Drop:
   Voltage Drop = V(start) - V(end)

4. In a closed circuit:
   Total Voltage Supplied = Total Voltage Consumed
